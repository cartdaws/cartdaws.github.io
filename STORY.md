# INTRUDER: the story

This is canon. Every line in the game should agree with it. Written from what the game actually does, so each story beat is something the player does or sees.

**In one sentence:** the humans emptied your valley one person at a time and took the elder last, so you steal their weapons by touching them, steal their ship, and chase them across space.

## The rule that holds it all together

**What we touch, we keep.** Your people write nothing down. Memory passes by touch, body to body, and the oldest one carries everyone. Anything you touch for long enough becomes part of you.

That one idea explains all of the gameplay:

| What the player does | Why it makes sense |
|---|---|
| Palms meet with the elder | It is how your people say everything |
| Absorb the sidearm, arm becomes a weapon | You touched it, so you keep it |
| Fly a human ship with no training | You touch the controls and you know them |
| Absorb robots, get stronger, learn forms | Same thing, and they carry memories you can read |
| Each weapon changes the arm | The body becomes what it learned |

It is also why the humans want you: a soldier who can learn any weapon by holding it. And why they want the elder most: it holds the memory of the whole species.

## Who is who

- **You.** Unnamed. The last one left in the valley. The ship logs you as **Specimen 09**.
- **The elder.** The one who stayed with you, and who carries everyone. **Specimen 08.** Speaks once in Chapter One, through the mark on the door.
- **The others.** Your six neighbours' households: **Specimens 01 to 07**, taken over the past months, one at a time.
- **HALCYON.** The research vessel, and the cold AI that now runs it. Its machines are the enemy you fight.
- **MERIDIAN.** The warship that burned the valley. The crew moved onto it. It carries the specimens away.
- **LANDER.** The stolen ship's own computer. It only knows it is going home.

**The title:** the humans came into your valley uninvited. When you board their ship, their AI calls *you* the intruder.

## Chapter One: The Halcyon

1. **The valley.** Quiet now. The others walked out one by one and never came back. You walk to the elder and your palms meet.
2. **The attack.** The Meridian comes down, burns the settlement and lifts the elder out in a beam. You are knocked flat.
3. **The ruins.** One of their landers crashed. *So they can fall.* You touch the dead pilot's sidearm and your arm becomes the Shardcaster. From it you learn their word for themselves: human.
4. **The lander.** You board. The computer says the pilot is not responding and it is flying home to the Halcyon. *Then take me there.*
5. **The flight.** The Halcyon challenges Lander Four, then sends fighters. You salvage homing barbs from the wrecks. Close in, it notices the pilot has no pulse and something else is flying. It calls off the fighters: *let it dock.* It wants you alive.
6. **The gas.** No guards, no alarms. Bay Three seals and fills with anesthetic. The last thing you hear: *Specimen Nine secured. Awaiting collection.*
7. **The mark.** You wake sooner than they planned. Nine. So eight went before you. On the bulkhead door is a smear you recognise: the elder's hand, dragged across it. You touch it and hear the elder: they are taking me to the hangar, deck nine, a great ship is waiting. Touch everything. Keep everything. Come for me.
8. **The deck.** You shoot the jammed lock and fight through the Robotics Deck to the hangar. Every machine you absorb gives up a memory, and those tell you what happened here: the crew left, why they want you, what Trial 27 was. In a sample store you find a piece of someone you knew and take it with you (the Lash).
9. **Too late.** The hangar lift reads out: the Meridian has undocked with all eight. The Halcyon tells you nothing is left aboard for you. *Nothing but a hangar full of ships.*

## Machine memories, in order

One plays each time you absorb a machine. They persist across deaths and reset on New Game.

1. All crew transferred to the Meridian. The machines will run the Halcyon from here.
2. Survey note: they keep nothing written. Every memory passes by touch, from one body to the next.
3. Seven specimens recovered from the valley so far. None of them will speak to us. None of them can.
4. Trial twenty-seven. The subject touched the scanner, and the scanner stopped being ours.
5. Command is very interested. A soldier that learns any weapon by holding it.
6. Order from the Meridian: bring the old one. It carries the memory of every one of them.
7. Specimen Eight secured. Meridian cleared to load all specimens for transfer.
8. Note added by Halcyon: it is reading these. Stop letting it touch the units.

## Where it goes next (not built)

- **Chapter Two:** you leave in a stolen ship. Space-pirate loop: raid human convoys for parts and leads on the Meridian.
- **The eight:** each specimen is a person to find. What is left of them after the trials is the open question.
- **The cost:** the more machines you keep, the less of you there is. Will the elder still know you?

## Rules for writing

- Your thoughts are short, plain and physical. No speeches.
- You do not know human words until you have touched human things. Before the sidearm it is "they" and "their".
- The Halcyon never helps you. It is polite, and that is what makes it unpleasant.
- The elder is not gentle: "You came. I asked you not to."
- Only machines and the elder explain things. You just react.
